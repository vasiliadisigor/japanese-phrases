      Reading Japanese out loud

      は as the topic particle is read wa: こんにちは, 実は, おすすめは. を is read o: 水をください. A small っ doubles the next consonant: 行ってきます is it-te-ki-ma-su. Long vowels matter: ありがとう is arigatō, not arigato. In です and ます the final u is often almost silent: des, mas.

      The original post marks a stressed syllable in red. Japanese actually uses pitch (high and low), not stress, and it changes with region and phrase. Copy the audio rather than a marked syllable.

      Casual or polite?

      Each card shows its register. Casual is for friends and peers. Polite (です／ます) is your default with clients, agencies, staff and anyone you have just met. Very polite is respectful keigo for clients, galleries and hotels. Slang is fine among friends, risky at work. Many casual phrases have a polite twin in the note.

      Install, audio, speaking & your progress

      iPhone: open the published HTTPS link in Safari, then Share → Add to Home Screen. On desktop, use the browser’s install option or a bookmark.

      Tap a speaker to hear Japanese. Choose a Japanese voice in settings. If audio is unavailable, use “Listen on the web”. That opens Google Translate; tap its speaker there.

      Speaking practice uses your browser’s speech recognition when it exists (Chrome, Edge, Safari). It checks the words it heard, not your pitch accent. Recognition sometimes writes a word in kanji where we use kana. If you know you said it right, tap “Count it as right”. Without recognition, you say it aloud and check yourself.

      Progress stays in this browser on this device. It does not sync. Correct cards return after 1, 3, 7, then 14 days. A mistake resets the card and brings it back in the same session. Phrases you miss twice or more appear in “Tricky phrases”. Use settings to back up and restore.
